# Product Emergence Skill

## Purpose
Move from idea to validated, launchable product through structured discovery, validation, and go-to-market strategy. Zero assumptions. Real customer feedback.

**The Reality (2026):**
- 42% of startups fail due to "no market need"
- 78% of B2B deals require $10K+ MRR or 1000+ users
- Companies that iterate within 30 days of launch are 3x more likely to achieve product-market fit
- **You must talk to 50 real customers BEFORE writing code**

---

## The Framework: 5 Phases (Emergence → Validation → Launch)

```
Phase 1: Emerge the Idea (Ikigai + JTBD)
    ↓
Phase 2: Validate the Problem (Talk to 50 customers)
    ↓
Phase 3: Design the Solution (MVP definition)
    ↓
Phase 4: Go-to-Market (Launch strategy)
    ↓
Phase 5: Market Validation (Early user feedback)
```

**Timeline:** 90 days from idea to first paying customers (or pivot)

---

## Phase 1: Emerge the Idea (Ikigai + JTBD)

### The Ikigai Quadrant

Find your product at the intersection of 4 elements:

```
                 ┌─────────────┐
                 │  YOUR PASSION
                 │  What you care about
                 │  What energizes you
                 └─────┬───────┘
                       │
        ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
        │              │              │
    ┌───▼────┐     ┌───▼────┐    ┌──▼────┐
    │ PASSION│     │ IKIGAI  │    │ MARKET│
    │        │     │ PRODUCT │    │ NEED  │
    │ + SKILL│     │ ↓       │    │       │
    └────────┘     └────┬────┘    └───┬───┘
        │               │             │
        │     ┌─────────┼─────────┐   │
        │     │         │         │   │
        └─────┴────┬────┴────┬────┴───┘
                   │         │
            ┌──────▼─┐   ┌───▼──────┐
            │ YOUR   │   │ MARKET   │
            │ TALENT │   │ WILL PAY │
            │ SKILLS │   │ FOR THIS │
            └────────┘   └──────────┘
```

### The 4 Quadrants

**1. Your Passion** (What you care about)
```
Ask yourself:
- What problems frustrate you?
- What could you talk about for hours?
- What would you work on without pay?

Example for Declic:
→ "I'm obsessed with making investing accessible"
```

**2. Your Talent** (What you're skilled at)
```
Ask yourself:
- What do people ask you for help with?
- What comes naturally to you?
- What are you better at than 90% of people?

Example for Declic:
→ "Portfolio strategy, risk analysis, algorithm building"
```

**3. Market Need** (What the world needs)
```
Ask yourself:
- What problems exist at scale?
- What are people currently paying for?
- What keeps people awake at night?

Example for Declic:
→ "1000s of people frustrated with portfolio management"
```

**4. Market Will Pay** (Viable business)
```
Ask yourself:
- Would someone pay $500/year for this?
- Is it a nice-to-have or must-have?
- Is the addressable market large enough?

Example for Declic:
→ "Yes: $2000+ annual cost of bad portfolio decisions"
```

### The Ikigai Output

```
If all 4 quadrants align:
✅ PASSION: You care deeply
✅ TALENT: You can build it
✅ NEED: Market problem exists
✅ VIABLE: People will pay

→ This is your product idea

If any quadrant is weak:
⚠️ CAUTION: Either pivot or don't build it
```

### Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD) Validation

Once you have an Ikigai idea, validate it with JTBD:

```
The Core Question: "What job are customers hiring your product to do?"

NOT: "Would you use a portfolio AI?"
BUT: "When you wake up worried about portfolio performance, 
what are you trying to accomplish?"

Answer patterns that matter:
- "I want to know if I'm on track for retirement" → Job: Peace of mind
- "I don't have time to manage it myself" → Job: Delegate management
- "I want to beat the S&P 500" → Job: Outperformance
- "I'm scared of making mistakes" → Job: Risk mitigation
```

**JTBD Interview Script:**

```
1. Context-setting question:
   "Tell me about the last time you felt frustrated 
   with your portfolio management."

2. Listen for the job (underlying motivation)

3. Probe for emotional core:
   "How did that make you feel?"
   "What would solving this mean for you?"

4. Confirm the job:
   "So essentially, you're trying to [job statement]?"
   
5. Understand urgency:
   "How important is this to solve?"
   "What are you doing about it today?"
```

**Interview Target:** 20-30 potential customers minimum

## Phase 2: Validate the Problem

### The 50-Customer Rule

Before you write a line of code:
```
Talk to 50 potential customers

Why 50?
- First 5-10: Exploratory (get context)
- 11-30: Validation (confirm patterns)
- 31-50: Refine understanding + find early adopters

Cost: ~100 hours of your time (2-3 hours per customer)
Value: Avoid building the wrong thing ($500K+ cost)
```

### Problem Validation Script (30 min call)

```
[0:00-2:00] RAPPORT
"Thanks for taking the time. I'm researching [area].
Your experience with [situation] is exactly what I need to understand."

[2:00-10:00] CONTEXT (Let them talk 80%)
"Can you walk me through what happened when [problem occurred]?"

Listen for:
- How they currently solve it
- Cost of current solution (time, money, frustration)
- Frequency (how often this problem occurs)

[10:00-15:00] EMOTIONAL CORE
"How did that frustrate you?"
"What's the impact on [their goal]?"

Listen for:
- Emotional intensity (indicates priority)
- Business impact ($, time lost, risk)

[15:00-20:00] VALIDATION
"So if I understood correctly, your main issue is [problem]?"

Listen for:
- Enthusiasm (did they light up?)
- Clarity (can they articulate it?)
- Consistency (same story across interviews?)

[20:00-25:00] DESIRABILITY TEST
"If you could wave a magic wand and [solve this],
what would that look like?"

Listen for:
- Specific solution ideas
- Willingness to change behavior
- Budget hints ("That's worth...")

[25:00-30:00] NEXT STEPS
"If I built [rough solution], would you use it?"
"Would you be willing to be an early user?"

Listen for:
- Commitment level (ask for email + permission to follow up)
```

### Problem Validation Checklist

After 50 interviews, you should have evidence:

```
✅ Problem exists at scale (heard it from 30+ people)
✅ Problem is urgent (people solving it today, imperfectly)
✅ Problem causes real pain (emotional + financial)
✅ Market is willing to solve it (they asked about solutions)
✅ You found 5-10 early adopters (willing to test)

If ANY of these is weak:
→ Pivot or abandon idea
```

## Phase 3: Design the Solution (MVP Definition)

### MVP Definition Framework

Once problem is validated, define the **Minimum Viable Product**:

```
Minimum: Smallest version that solves core problem
Viable: Actually works (not demo, not concept)
Product: Real value to customer

NOT a full product. NOT all features. 
JUST the core job your product does.
```

### The MVP Canvas

```
Problem Solved:
"Automated portfolio rebalancing without manual management"

Core Job:
"Help busy professionals delegate portfolio management"

MVP Feature Set:
1. Connect brokerage account (auth)
2. Analyze current portfolio (assessment)
3. Recommend rebalancing (AI engine)
4. Execute rebalancing (automation)
5. Report performance (dashboard)

NOT included in MVP:
- Tax optimization
- Advanced analytics
- Mobile app
- Advanced risk profiles
- Multi-account management
- International support

Who Benefits Most (ICP):
- Busy professionals
- $100K-$500K portfolios
- Want passive income
- No time for management

Success Metrics:
- Sign-up: 100+ in first month
- Activation: 80%+ complete setup
- Rebalancing: 90%+ accept recommendations
- Retention: 80%+ month-over-month

Build Time:
- 8-12 weeks with 2-3 engineers
- Cost: $50K-$100K

Timeline:
- Weeks 1-2: Infrastructure
- Weeks 3-8: Core features
- Weeks 9-10: Testing + edge cases
- Weeks 11-12: Launch prep
```

### Lean Development Approach

```
Week 1: Wireframes + user flows (no coding)
Week 2: Hi-fi mockups (click-through prototype)
Week 3: Validate mockups with 5 early users
Week 4-8: Build MVP (iterate weekly with users)
Week 9-10: Polish + bug fixes
Week 11: Soft launch to 10 early adopters
Week 12: Public launch + monitoring
```

## Phase 4: Go-to-Market Strategy

### Phase 4a: Pre-Launch (Weeks 11-12)

**Find 5-10 Early Adopters:**
```
Source:
- From your 50 customer interviews (they said yes!)
- LinkedIn outreach (target ICP)
- Beta list signup on landing page
- Product Hunt early access

Offer: Free access + direct access to you for feedback

Goal: Early feedback before public launch
```

**Launch Checklist:**

```
✅ Landing page (benefits + clear CTA)
✅ Product demo video (2 min)
✅ Early adopter email list (5-10 people)
✅ Setup documentation (get started guide)
✅ Support plan (direct Slack/email for beta users)
✅ Feedback loop (weekly sync with beta users)
✅ Metrics tracking (GA4 + product analytics)
```

### Phase 4b: Launch (Week 13)

**Launch Sequence:**

```
Day 1: Soft launch to 5-10 early adopters
  - Monitor for critical bugs
  - Respond to every question
  - Track usage patterns

Day 2-3: Expand to early adopter list (50+ people)
  - Share launch in relevant communities
  - Offer early-bird pricing if paid
  - Email warm list (network)

Day 4-7: Product Hunt / Tech community launch
  - Prepare for ~100-500 signups
  - Monitor server performance
  - Respond to comments/feedback

Week 2: Expand to paid if suitable
  - Early adopters at discounted "founder" rate
  - Gather willingness-to-pay data
  - Iterate based on feedback
```

### Phase 4c: Early User Validation (Weeks 13-16)

**Success Metrics (First Month):**

```
Acquisition:
- 100+ signups (validate demand)
- 5-10 paying customers at founder rate (validate viability)

Activation:
- 80%+ complete onboarding
- 70%+ use core feature

Retention:
- 80%+ come back week 2
- 60%+ come back week 4

Revenue:
- Average revenue per user (ARPU)
- Willingness to pay ($X/month viable?)

Feedback:
- NPS (Net Promoter Score) > 40
- Clear feature requests
- Problem-solution fit validation
```

**Feedback Loop:**

```
Weekly:
- Talk to 5 active users
- Ask: What's working? What's broken?
- Prioritize bugs vs features vs nice-to-haves

Bi-weekly:
- Analyze usage data
- Identify drop-off points
- Iterate product

Monthly:
- Assess product-market fit signals
- Decide: Pivot or double down?
```

## Phase 5: Product-Market Fit Validation

### The PMF Signals

You have product-market fit when:

```
✅ Retention: 70%+ month-over-month (people stay)
✅ Viral: Users bring new users (NPS > 50)
✅ Revenue: Clear path to profitability
✅ Demand: More demand than supply (waiting list)
✅ Organic Growth: Growing without heavy marketing
```

### The PMF Questions

Ask yourself:

```
1. "Would customers be very disappointed 
   if they could no longer use this?"
   YES = Strong PMF signal
   NO = Need to iterate

2. "Have we found a repeatable 
   way to acquire customers cheaply?"
   YES = Ready to scale
   NO = Adjust positioning/targeting

3. "Are customers asking for MORE features 
   or BETTER execution of existing features?"
   More features = PMF achieved (they want more)
   Better execution = Still iterating

4. "Can we reach profitability 
   at current unit economics?"
   YES = Scale
   NO = Adjust pricing/costs
```

---

## The Decision Tree: Pivot or Double Down

After 90 days (Phase 5):

```
                    ┌─ PMF Signals Strong ─→ SCALE (Double down)
Validation Data ────┤
                    ├─ PMF Signals Weak ────→ ITERATE (Small pivot)
                    │
                    └─ No Market Need ──────→ PIVOT or ABANDON
                    
Where to pivot:
❌ Wrong ICP? → Change target audience
❌ Wrong problem? → Find related problem
❌ Wrong solution? → Keep problem, redesign
❌ Wrong model? → Change pricing/delivery
❌ Bad timing? → Revisit in 6-12 months
```

**After Phase 1 (Emergence):**
- [ ] All 4 Ikigai quadrants align
- [ ] JTBD clearly articulated

- [ ] 50+ interviews completed
- [ ] Problem confirmed (30+ people mentioned it)
- [ ] 5-10 early adopters committed

**After Phase 3 (MVP):**

## Success Stories (The PMF Signals)

### What Good Looks Like
```
Week 1: 120 signups
Week 2: 97 still active (80% retention)
Week 3: 15 have paid (12% conversion)
Week 4: 120 new signups (organic + word-of-mouth)

→ This is PMF territory
→ Scale acquisition
```

### What to Worry About
```
Week 1: 200 signups
Week 2: 80 still active (40% retention)
Week 3: 2 have paid (1% conversion)
Week 4: 50 new signups (declining)

→ Problem: Either wrong ICP, solution isn't valuable, or both
→ Pivot or kill
```

6. **Success Metrics** (specific numbers, timeline)
7. **Pivot Decision Framework** (when to pivot vs scale)
